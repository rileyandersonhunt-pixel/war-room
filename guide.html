Field Guide

How To Run
The War Room

The War Room does three jobs: it lets you practice drafting against a room that behaves like real people, it coaches you after every draft, and on draft day it runs alongside your real draft as a co-pilot. This guide gets you from first click to draft-day ready.

Open The War Room

01Two Minutes To Your First Draft

Leave everything on default the first time. The defaults are a 12-team, half-PPR snake draft, picking 6th.

  1. Set Mode to Practice vs AI, match Teams, Scoring, and Your draft slot to your real league, and check the roster matches yours.
  2. Hit Start Mock Draft. The other eleven managers pick until it reaches you.
  3. When the amber bar says You are on the clock, click a player to draft him. That is the whole loop.
The one setting that matters Unpredictability defaults to True to market, which is tuned to match how real drafts actually spread (measured against 2,810 of them). Practice there. Slide toward Bedlam when you want reps against a chaotic room, but know that you chose chaos.

The Adjusted sort (the default) is ADP plus the docks: the "who should I actually take next" order. Flip to ADP any time to see the raw market. The header stamp shows the dates of the data you are looking at.

03The Advice Tab

The right-hand rail's first tab is your analyst. It never disagrees with the engine, because it is the engine.

  • Best Picks Right Now. Top five for your roster, ranked by what waiting costs: a player's value now minus what his position will realistically offer at your next pick, using the calibrated survival odds. That is why the last good tight end can outrank a better-named receiver on a deep shelf: position over player, when the math says so. Each card says the cost in plain terms ("waiting at TE costs about 2.2 pts") along with the usual reasons. Click one to draft him straight from the list.
  • Safe To Wait On. Players you like who will probably still be there next turn. This list is how you stop burning picks on players you could have had a round later.
  • Tier Cliffs. How many players remain in the current tier at each position, and where the next tier's price starts. When a position you still need shows 1 left in red, that is the moment to decide, not two picks later.
  • Run alerts. When five of the last eight picks are one position, a banner says so. Runs end; the value they leave behind at other positions does not.

While other managers pick, the tab becomes a watchlist: your likely targets, each with live odds of surviving to your turn. Plan while you wait; picking is then just confirming the plan.

04Practicing With Intent

Random reps make you familiar. Deliberate reps make you better.

  • Rewind is your A/B test. Click any completed pick on the Board (or in the Feed) to rewind there and choose differently. The room keeps the same instincts, so the comparison is honest: same managers, same tendencies, different decision. Rewinding your round 3 pick and finishing both ways teaches more than three fresh drafts.
  • Seeds replay a room. Same seed and settings recreate the identical draft room. Shuffle the seed for a fresh one. Found a room that punished you? Keep the seed and beat it.
  • The Room tab shows each opponent's strategy: Zero RB, Early QB, Value Vulture and so on. Reading the room mid-draft ("three teams still need a QB before my next pick") is a real skill this trains.
  • Your own rankings. Paste your board into setup, one name per line. A blank line or a line saying "Tier 2" starts a new tier. The list gains a My board sort, and every row shows a MY chip: green when the market prices your guy below your rank (you can wait on him), red when you would have to reach. Drafting your board against the market's prices is the entire skill of drafting.
Keyboard Type in the search box and press Enter to draft the top match. Esc clears the search. b toggles the board, s sims to your pick, z rewinds.

05After The Draft: The Coaching

The grade is the least important thing on the results screen. Read upward from the bottom.

  • The grade scores your starters' value over replacement against the other rosters. It measures how well you shopped, not how many points you will score. An A means you captured value and scarcity; it cannot know whether your bets hit.
  • Pick-By-Pick Review is the real coaching, and it judges opportunity cost, not names. Every pick gets a verdict: clean the right sequence at that moment, including "right position at the right time" when you beat a cliff by passing a better name. value left the other sequence was better even after counting scarcity, and it tells you when that player actually went. could have waited your guy probably survived to your next turn AND his shelf was deep behind him. conviction your pick was backed by a riser flag (a coach naming a starter, a role won in camp). The sim tracks these instead of judging them; your reads are yours.
  • Coach Notes turn the review into habits: QB timing versus the room, bye-week stacking, kicker timing.
  • Your Draft Log saves every finished draft in this browser. After three or more, the setup screen starts naming your patterns, like taking a QB a round and a half before the room does. When a habit stops appearing in the log, you fixed it.

06Five Drills That Actually Transfer

  1. The patience rep. Finish a draft with zero "could have waited" verdicts. Use Safe To Wait On aggressively. This is the fastest grade improvement available.
  2. The cliff rep. Draft a full mock making every positional decision at the moment a tier cliff turns red, never after. You are training when to decide.
  3. The slot tour. Run the same room (same seed) from pick 1, pick 6, and pick 12. The turn (1.01) and the wheel (1.12) are different sports; do not let your real draft be your first rep from your real slot.
  4. The run rep. Set unpredictability to Bedlam and survive a positional run without chasing it. Panic is a trainable reflex.
  5. The A/B rep. After each draft, rewind to your most uncertain pick, take the other guy, and finish again. Compare the two reviews, not the two grades.

07Draft Day: Live Co-Pilot

Switch Mode to Live draft co-pilot. Nobody auto-picks. You mirror your real draft into the War Room and it analyzes everything in return.

  • Entering picks is the whole job. When anyone in your real draft picks, type three or four letters, hit Enter, done. The search clears itself for the next pick. It takes about two seconds per pick once you have the rhythm.
  • Fixing mistakes. Undo pick takes back the last entry. For an error a few picks back, click that pick in the Feed and re-enter from there.
  • Name the managers. In the Room tab, click any team name to rename it to the real manager. Now "Big Mike already has three receivers" is knowledge you own mid-draft.
  • Between your picks, the Advice tab shows your targets with live survival odds. On your pick, it is the same Best Picks Right Now you practiced with.
  • When it ends (or if your league only drafts partway), hit Exit and get the full analysis of your real draft, saved to your log like any other.

Draft-day checklist

  • Use the same device and browser you practiced on. Your log and autosave live there.
  • Hard-refresh the page once before the draft starts (Cmd+Shift+R) so you are on the newest data. Check the date stamp in the header.
  • Set mode to Live, match teams, scoring, roster, and your real slot exactly.
  • Paste your final rankings into Your Board.
  • Run one two-minute practice draft in the morning to warm up the entry rhythm.
If anything crashes The draft autosaves on every pick. Reopen the page and a Resume button restores everything: every pick, your renamed managers, whoever is on the clock. You cannot lose a draft to a refresh.

08What It Knows And What It Does Not

  • It knows the market: real ADP from thousands of drafts, in your scoring format, plus injury, practice, and role reporting with dates on every note. The header stamp tells you how fresh.
  • The docks are editorial. They size the risk from sourced facts, but they are judgment. The chip and note give you everything you need to overrule one, and you should when you know better.
  • It does not project points. No projections, no schedule analysis, no weekly matchups. It prices scarcity and value; whether a player is good is still your call.
  • It is a snapshot, not a feed. The page updates when a new version is published and you reload it. It cannot pull news on its own.
  • Your data stays yours. The log and autosave live in your browser only. Nothing you do in the app is sent anywhere.
